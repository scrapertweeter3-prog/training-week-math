150 minutes has to come from somewhere

Zone 2 gets sold as a feeling: conversational pace, nose breathing, fat burn. The actual unit is minutes. The standard floor is 150 a week of moderate cardio, and that floor holds no matter how the week is split or what the calendar already holds. A lifter's week already holds four sessions at an hour each, and the 150 lands on top of real training, a commute, and sleep. Reading the protocol before running it is how HackedSelf handles every claim, and the training week deserves the same treatment: the dose is the purchase, the minutes are the price.

The week pays for the dose first

Run the numbers on a standard lifting week. Four sessions at sixty minutes is 240 minutes of lifting. The cardio floor adds 150 more, which lands the week at 390 minutes of training. Spread across six training days that is 65 minutes a day, every day, with one full rest day. Try to hide the cardio inside the four lifting days instead and each one runs about 97 minutes. A 97-minute session is a different commitment than a 60-minute one, and it is the reason most lifters drop the cardio block by week three.

Only a few honest layouts exist

Append thirty minutes to two lifting sessions and put three half-hour blocks on off days: two sessions stretch to 90 minutes, three short ones carry the rest, and the week touches training seven days out of seven, which is its own cost. A cleaner split leaves lifting days untouched: three fifty-minute zone 2 rides on non-lifting days cover the floor at 150 exactly and every lift stays at sixty. What does not exist is a layout where the minutes cost nothing. The floor is 150 either way; the layouts only decide who feels it.

Missed doses wait on the books

The floor also compounds in reverse. Skip cardio for a month and the debt is 600 minutes. Paying it back at thirty minutes a session is twenty sessions, which is seven weeks of catch-up at three sessions a week. Almost nobody runs that schedule, so the skipped month drifts into a skipped quarter, and the lifters who announce they don't do cardio are usually carrying two years of it. A missed dose does not expire. It waits like any other balance.

The sauna shortcut gets read like a claim

One substitution is tempting enough to audit. The site's own sauna protocol piece sets the cardiovascular floor at four sessions above 175°F, and if that block genuinely stands in for zone 2, the week buys its cardio in four visits instead of 150 minutes of steady pace. Whether the swap holds is an evidence question first, and the answer would rewrite the arithmetic above. Until it is settled, the minutes are owed the old way: on the calendar, at about 65 a day.
